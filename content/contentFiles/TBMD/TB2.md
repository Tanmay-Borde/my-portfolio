# The Future
It’s 2050, imagine a “Wirelessly Powered World” where technology simply works. Invisibly. Seamlessly. Wirelessly. It evolves from a utility tool we use into a sophisticated, self-powered extension of ourselves. Just as wireless connectivity revolutionized how devices communicate, wireless charging transforms how they’re powered.
The future of wireless power transmission (“Air Charging”), lets power flow through spaces. Portable devices self-charge and self-coordinate, turning living rooms, workspaces, and cafes into an ambient, always-on power ecosystem. Think of it as a ubiquitous Wi-Fi-like network, but for charging.
I call this the Ambient Power Ecosystem (APE): an invisible, background wireless-power field that trickle-charges devices automatically and keeps them powered all the time. In this future, technology recedes into the environment and becomes a natural interface with the world, so deeply integrated it feels indistinguishable from magic. In the APE world, wireless power is omnipresent and you don’t have to care about charging your devices ever, especially with the old clunky wires!

## Human-Technology Interface redefined
The best interface is no interface. The North Star of the Human-Technology Interface (HTI) is simple: make technology so intuitive it becomes an inherent and symbiotic part of us. Embedded so well into everyday objects, it no longer feels like an electronic gadget. It blurs the line between human intent and machine agency, extending artificial cognition as our natural peripheral. That’s Ambient Computing, the paradigm shift in HTI, enabled by AI.

## Ambient Computing needs Ambient Power
Ambient computing isn’t just about Hardware-Software; it requires a reliable, always-available energy layer as well. AR smart glasses, mixed-reality headsets, and other AI-enabled personal wearables are the ideal form factors to unlock always-on, context-aware Artificial Intelligence, provided they’re freed from constant plug-in charging. Air charging enables that freedom.
In this world, wearables and portable AI hardware of any form factor are no longer constrained by wires, charging hassle, or battery anxiety. They’re powered invisibly Over-The-Air (OTA) by a sophisticated air-charging ecosystem working silently in the background delivering a truly unconstrained, always-ready user experience.
Yes! You can imagine this as Jarvis from Iron Man, but instead of an arc reactor, it will be powered by Air Charging Technology.

# The Present
Let’s come back to today and see how we’re actually heading to that future. It’s 2025: AI is everywhere, hardware teams are sprinting, and everyone wants the “post-smartphone” moment. But the smartphone isn’t disappearing tomorrow. Here’s the honest read on why and how the transition really unfolds.
- It’s the primary PC: As of today, the smartphone is the central device for 5.2B+ people. It’s a personal key that holds your identity, context, compute, and connectivity. It quietly enables everything around you—fitness trackers, smart home devices and even your car (CarPlay/Android Auto).
Near term reality: AI hardware like smart AR glasses will certainly offload a lot (navigation, capture, glanceable info) and cut screen time, but they won’t replace the phone outright. Think of them as a heads-up companion to the device already in your pocket.
- Platform & Distribution: Every failed AI Hardware product so far had one thing in common: “They were directly replacing the smartphone head-on without any existing leverage of distribution”. Any standalone AI gadget completely decoupled from the smartphone instantly becomes a friction for average consumers making market penetration a big challenge.
Platform giants like Apple with iPhone, Google with Android, OpenAI with chatGPT and Meta with Instagram have a leg up in distribution and branding, yet they’re building AI hardware that is fundamentally a phone-paired peripheral, not standalone replacements. It’s the practical path to mainstream adoption.
- Display matters: An average consumer is habituated to a display interface and need visual cues and live feedback to effectively interact with the software. Meta recently launched Ray Ban Display (Gen-2) and gesture control Neural Band in Sept’25 that is the best and most sophisticated AI enabled smart AR glasses so far. It’s sleek, compact and light in weight, weighing just ~70 grams, making it the only practically wearable smart glasses in the market with augmented display.
- Cost & adoption curve: Smartphones benefit from brutal scale and mature supply chains. If we go by “The Law of diffusion for Innovation”—Wearables are earlier on the S-curve: innovators and early adopters are pulling the category forward; broader audiences follow as utility rises and costs fall. Meanwhile, wireless-charging accessories are already drifting into early-majority behavior because they make everyday life easier without changing habits.
- It’s a phased transition: As Steve Jobs quoted “Technology goes in cycles like spring, summer and autumn before entering the graveyard of technology”. Right now, smart wearables + AI are in “spring,” and smartphones are somewhere in “summer/autumn”—not in the graveyard ‘yet’!

Having said that, this doesn't mean smartphone will never be replaced, it will be at some point in time in future and nobody including the experts can predict ‘when’, but we do have clear understanding of ‘how’. There is already a paradigm shift happening in consumer electronics, the industry is moving from portable devices you carry to personal devices you wear. But the biggest engineering and practical utility challenge of smart wearables is “battery”.

- Hardware: like semiconductors and electronics advance on the order of months. This is where charging mobility enabled by Wireless Charging comes into the picture that makes powering your portable and wearable devices significantly better, hassle free, convenient and of course, futuristic!

## Wireless Charging: shovel in the AI gold rush
AI hardware is exploding, and capital is flooding into the space—but one foundational technology is still underweighted: wireless charging. It redefines how consumer AI products are powered. It’s no longer a “premium or high-end feature”; mainstream hardware manufacturers now prioritize wire-free charging as must have feature, and the market is already ~$30.75B (2024).
But, what you see today in cars, offices, IKEA furniture, airports, and cafés is not “air charging.” It’s contact/near-field wireless charging. So how do we get to the wireless future from the opening of this blog? The transition is already underway—in two phases:
### Wireless Charging 1.0 (Wired → Wireless)
This is the current phase we are in, driven by inductive and resonant technologies, 1.0 uses electromagnetic fields at short range to charge compatible devices. It dominates consumer electronics, automotive, and medical use cases, thanks to the de-facto standard: Qi (pronounced “chee”).
The latest version Qi-2.2 (2025) solves biggest efficiency problem by bringing stronger magnets with precise charging coil alignment enabling 25w fast wireless charging speed. Qi versions promises incremental annual gains with better efficiency, thermal management, charging speed and technology advancements.
The wireless charging accessories market is fragmented and cluttered with many Chinese accessories that even support 80w fast wireless charging. However, Qi stands out as the globally trusted and recognized wireless charging standard adopted & contributed by tech giants like Apple, Google & Samsung.
Qi commands 82% market share, $9.56 Bn market value with 24.4% CAGR, 11k+ Qi-certified products and 1.5 Bn+ active devices compatible with Qi (2024). Today, 70% of smartphone, 68% of smartwatches and all other mainstream consumer electronics by default support Qi-Wireless Charging. Here’s a quick look at the Qi adoption in B2B and B2C markets:
- **Public Infrastructure:** 480,000+ Qi charging points deployed globally across airports (23%), hotels (18%), retail spaces (34%) and others (25%) as of 2024.
- **Automotive Industry:** More than 78% of 2025 EV models and most of the ICE cars include Qi wireless charging consoles, although most of them are not Qi2, resulting in less efficiency & slower charging speed, but at-least it’s accelerating consumer awareness and habit formation.
- **Medical Devices:** 92% of FCC-approved wireless medical equipment uses Qi for sterilizable charging.
- **Logistics Automation:** Amazon warehouses reduced AGV downtime by 62% using floor-embedded Qi pads for robots.
- **Retail Solutions:** IKEA's 2025 furniture line embeds Qi2 in 34% of products, enabling 15W wireless charging furnitures for homes & workspaces.

### Wireless Charging 2.0 (Wireless → Over-the-Air)
This is the “Wireless Future” we’re aiming for: driven by the Radio Frequency (RF) and Infrared (IR) based over-the-air wireless power transmission (WPT) technology. Power is sent through space—charging devices at a distance. Air Charging Technology already exists and many key players like Motorola, Xiaomi, Energous, GuRu, AirFuel Alliance & WiTricity are extensively working on advancing this tech, some have even received the FCC & FDA approval for real-time mW to ~1W WPT to charge ultra low powered devices up to 10 meters. RF & IR WPT are the two most promising technologies for the Air Charging future and both are serving two different use cases:
- IR-WPT (optical/infrared): great for stationary low powered endpoints—smart locks, doorbells, cameras, fixed IoT—where line-of-sight is fine. Highly directional beams offer strong power density and clean installs (no cabling). This tech has better power density compared to RF-WPT and it can focus maximum energy transmission to the receiver device in the line-of-sight of IR Power Transmitter. In May25’ DARPA program has set a record by transmitting 800W of power at 8.6 kms wirelessly over-the-air in 30 seconds using the optical power beaming. Retail pilots (e.g., cordless in-store digital signage) are already proving out the ops benefits.
- RF-WPT (radio): better for portable/wearable low powered devices—smart glasses, AR/VR headsets, smart rings, drones and small moving robots. RF-WPT creates an Ambient Power Field that uses phased-array antennas beaming RF (e.g., 2.4 GHz/5.8 GHz), enabling multi-device top-ups with spatial freedom (no strict line-of-sight). Today efficiencies are lower than near-field, but the pace of improvement—plus emerging regulatory clarity makes RF a strong candidate for room-scale “always-on” trickle charging (mW to ~W classes), ideal for sensors and intermittently active wearables.

Why momentum now? The rise of AI glasses and wearables. Shipments are up triple digits YoY, and as the form factor matures, power becomes the bottleneck. That pressure is pulling investment into air-charging: the inevitable frontier that removes charging rituals from compact, space-constrained devices.

## Air Charging for Smart Wearables
Take a look at this teardown of state-of-the-art display smart glasses (Gen-2):
![Meta Display Gen2 Teardown](/content/images/meta_display_teardown.png)
As highlighted, battery size & weight dominates the design by occupying more than 50% internals of the space constrained smart glasses. It leaves less room for other hardware components, compromises design choices and it’s the only major internal component that degrades faster compared to others. With incremental upgrades, the feature set keeps expanding: always-on camera and microphones, continuous connectivity, display rendering, and on-device AI.
**Net effect:** constant power requirement draw goes up while we’re trying to make the product lighter and smaller for maximum wearability.
This is the design paradox of smart glasses today—keep a big battery (heavier, hotter, thicker), move it off the head (tethers and pockets), or throttle features (compromise the experience). None of these feel like the future.

Air charging changes the equation. By externalizing power into the environment with wireless power transmission (WPT), we build an Ambient Power Ecosystem (APE) that enables:
- **A small on-device buffer** that’s topped up in APE zones (home, office, cafés, transit)—no swaps (Just like a Wi-Fi).
- **Lower weight and more internal space** for optics, sensors, and compute—while retaining true wearability.
- **Power as a background service**—ambient top-ups where you live, work, and move.
- **The experience becomes truly ambient**—you never care how the technology is powered.

Near-term reality: buffer battery + ambient trickle charging (mW→W depending on distance and tech). As coverage density, efficiency, and live safety telemetry improve, certain workloads (notifications, glanceable HUD, sensors) trend toward battery-optional designs. The point isn’t “no battery ever”; it’s no charging ritual—and a form factor that finally feels like eyewear, not a gadget strapped to your face.

This is a glimpse of the next 25 years, the transition to a “Wirelessly Powered World” where technology simply works. Invisibly. Seamlessly. Wirelessly. The idea dates back to the genius of Nikola Tesla who dreamt this in 1901 and it will become a living reality in the 21st century.
In the coming decade, the core engineering challenge is hardware miniaturization under tight space & power constraint, thanks to the paradigm shift of smart AI wearables. With Year-over-year advancements in semiconductors, electronics & RF, chips are delivering more compute per milliwatt; power-management silicon is cooler and smarter; and wireless power is moving from pads to spaces.
As this stack matures, battery-less smart wearables powered by Ambient Power Ecosystem is the inevitable frontier. Wireless Charging is here to stay, people evolved in this future won’t “charge a device” any more than they “connect to the internet”—it will just be there. When this Human-Technology Interface becomes a reality, we’re no longer limited by power. We’re limited only by our intent & imagination.
